# v0.3.0 qualification — policy, authorization, approval, and plan review

This document is the acceptance/evidence map for the v0.3.0 Experimental authority-review milestone. Until the milestone is implemented and the referenced evidence is produced by the protected release process, this document is a **qualification specification**, not proof that the capability is released.

## Claim boundary

v0.3.0 may claim only that Linura can take its canonical evidence-bound non-executable reconciliation plan through deterministic trusted risk classification, policy and approval review while preserving authenticated-principal binding and the non-mutation boundary.

It may not claim durable prepare/recovery, executor integration, managed external mutation, post-effect verification, complete lifecycle execution, complete risk coverage for future domains, a supported platform profile or production readiness.

## Required evidence groups

### 1. Canonical plan lineage

Prove that policy review consumes a subject derived from `linura-planner::ReconciliationPlan` and does not revive provider-owned planning or the removed bootstrap `ActionPlan` path.

Required checks:

- no public/in-repo `ActionRequest`, `PlanResponse`, generic `Provider::plan`, `ControlPlane::apply` or `MutationRuntime` authority path remains;
- policy review projection is created from the canonical reconciliation plan plus authenticated principal identity;
- trusted risk classification is owned by Control and consumes canonical typed plan material rather than client/model/provider risk claims;
- providers remain bounded observation mechanisms and do not own policy/approval orchestration;
- D-Bus remains authentication/wire adaptation only.

### 2. Deterministic risk classification and policy outcomes

The planner's `prospective_risk` is treated as a lower bound. Required positive/negative cases:

- canonical systemd `active_state` change is elevated to `SecuritySensitive` by the baseline trusted risk rule;
- canonical security-sensitive work reaches administrator approval;
- a canonical plan classified `Destructive` by a trusted test rule reaches destructive-action approval without claiming a supported destructive mutation rule;
- unmatched/unclassified canonical mutation becomes `blocked`;
- risk downgrade below the planner floor is rejected and becomes `blocked`;
- overlapping rules choose the highest risk deterministically;
- the same normalized plan and risk-policy revision/rules produce the same classification;
- `allow` for a permitted subject;
- explicit `deny`;
- `require-approval` for interactive-user class;
- `require-approval` for administrator class;
- `require-approval` for destructive-action class;
- `blocked` for a plan containing blockers/unknown required state;
- unknown/unsupported policy input fails closed rather than defaulting to allow.

### 3. Exact review binding

Every policy evaluation and approval check must prove binding to:

- authenticated principal;
- request ID;
- plan ID;
- material canonical planned changes/findings;
- authoritative evidence ID;
- provider/resource/capability identity;
- semantic provenance;
- planner risk floor and classified prospective risk;
- risk-policy revision and matched rule identities;
- policy ID/revision;
- approval requirement/evidence when applicable.

Negative cases must reject substitution of each materially security-relevant field. A plan ID by itself is not sufficient authority evidence.

### 4. Approval lifecycle

When approval is required, evidence must prove:

- approver identity is authenticated rather than client-asserted;
- the approver satisfies the required class/constraint;
- an agent cannot approve its own mutation proposal;
- approval for one principal cannot be stolen by another principal;
- approval for one plan/evidence/policy or risk-policy revision cannot authorize another;
- issuance and expiry are explicit;
- expired approval is rejected;
- revoked approval is rejected;
- replay after a material review-binding change is rejected;
- missing or malformed approval fails closed.

### 5. Explainability and provenance

A reviewed result must retain enough typed information to explain:

- who/what requested the change (`Actor` provenance);
- which authenticated principal owns the review;
- why the desired state exists (semantic origin);
- what material state changes/findings were reviewed;
- which authoritative evidence was used;
- which trusted risk-policy revision/rules classified the plan and what risk resulted;
- which policy snapshot produced the decision;
- whether approval was required and what evidence satisfied it.

Model-generated prose is not authoritative policy or risk-classification evidence.

### 6. Resource and hostile-input bounds

Every new public request, retained review and approval representation must define and test bounded:

- identifier/string sizes;
- number of findings/changes/origins;
- approval metadata size;
- retained entry count;
- per-entry and aggregate retention bytes;
- malformed/duplicate fields;
- deterministic eviction behavior if process-local retention is used.

Risk classification and policy evaluation must be deterministic, bounded and free of unbounded polling, retries or background work.

### 7. Non-execution proof

Unit/integration tests and exact-source VM acceptance must prove all of the following even after a plan receives an `allow` result or valid approval:

- reviewed plan remains explicitly non-executable;
- no executor handle is returned;
- no Polkit authorization is requested;
- no durable prepare record is created;
- no shell/generic privileged command path is invoked;
- native Linux state remains unchanged;
- v0.2 plan-preview regression cases remain green.

The strongest system assertion is independent re-observation/native inspection of the controlled fixture before and after review/approval.

### 8. Security/threat-model evidence

Tests/review must cover at minimum:

- approval replay;
- stale-plan/stale-evidence approval;
- policy substitution/downgrade;
- risk-policy substitution/downgrade;
- client/model/provider attempts to assert a weaker risk;
- unclassified mutation defaulting to a weaker class;
- cross-principal approval theft;
- agent self-approval;
- approver-class escalation;
- confused deputy behavior;
- forged client actor/principal identity;
- TOCTOU between plan review and any later milestone's prepare boundary;
- malicious retrieved/model context attempting to influence authority.

`docs/threat-model.md` and ADR 0018 are normative review inputs.

## Expected system qualification

The v0.3 exact-source disposable VM scenario should extend the v0.2 Control1 plan-preview fixture rather than create a separate provider-owned planning path. At minimum it must:

1. authenticate a local caller;
2. observe the controlled native resource authoritatively;
3. produce a deterministic change-proposed plan preview;
4. classify the canonical plan with the trusted risk policy and retain classification provenance;
5. obtain the expected deterministic policy result;
6. exercise the required-approval boundary where applicable: the real daemon/CLI VM path must prove the required approval class, while authenticated approval-evidence issuance/expiry/revocation remains qualified by exact-source deterministic Control tests until a trusted human interaction adapter exists; service D-Bus credentials must never be promoted into human/admin approval authority;
7. retrieve/explain the reviewed result;
8. test at least one binding mismatch/replay rejection;
9. test an unclassified/downgrade risk rejection in deterministic authority tests;
10. prove every public result remains non-executable;
11. independently re-observe/native-inspect the resource and prove it did not change.

The existing authoritative-observation and plan-preview VM scenarios remain mandatory regressions.

## Release evidence

Final release qualification must bind evidence to the exact selected source SHA and follow Linura's normal trusted release process. Human prose in this file does not override machine-generated CI, VM, proof, artifact or publication results.
