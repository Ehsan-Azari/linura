# v0.3.0 — policy, authorization, approval, and plan review

**Status:** implementation complete; publication pending protected release qualification
**Target claim class:** Experimental

## Goal

Complete Linura's authority-side **plan review** boundary on top of the v0.2 canonical deterministic reconciliation plan without introducing supported external mutation.

The target path is:

```text
authenticated semantic request
→ authoritative observation
→ deterministic ReconciliationPlan
→ structural validation
→ trusted deterministic risk classification
→ authenticated policy-review subject
→ deterministic policy evaluation
→ approval requirement / approval evidence when required
→ reviewed-plan result
```

The milestone stops before durable prepare, execution, post-effect verification, commit, mutation audit and reconciliation. A reviewed or approved plan remains non-executable at the public product boundary.

## Canonical plan lineage

v0.3 extends the v0.2 `linura-planner::ReconciliationPlan` lineage. It must not revive or create a competing provider-owned/executable planning model.

The superseded bootstrap path `ActionRequest → Provider::plan → ActionPlan → ControlPlane::apply` is not part of this milestone. Policy receives a review projection created by Linura from the canonical reconciliation plan plus authenticated principal identity. Clients, providers and models cannot author that projection independently.

## Required capabilities

1. `linura-policy` consumes a canonical review subject derived from `ReconciliationPlan`, not provider-generated effects or transport data.
2. Policy outcomes are deterministic and typed: `allow`, `deny`, `require-approval`, or `blocked`.
3. Blocked/invalid plans cannot be promoted into approval merely because a caller asks for review.
4. The planner's `prospective_risk` is a risk floor. Control performs deterministic trusted risk classification before policy review and may raise, but never lower, that floor.
5. Unclassified mutation risk fails closed as `blocked`; it must never inherit an ordinary mutation approval class by default.
6. Trusted classification binds to typed canonical plan material. Clients/models/providers cannot assert, lower or bypass the authority risk.
7. Overlapping trusted rules resolve deterministically to the highest risk. Risk-policy revision and matched rule identities remain material review provenance.
8. Review binds the authenticated principal to the exact request, plan, authoritative evidence identity, provider, resource, capability, classified risk, risk-policy provenance and policy snapshot/revision.
9. Policy and approval explanations preserve semantic reason/provenance and the material planned changes being reviewed.
10. Approval requirements define the required approval class and explicit approver constraints; unsupported/unknown requirements fail closed.
11. Approval evidence is authenticated and bound to the exact review binding. A changed plan, evidence identity, principal, policy revision, risk-policy classification, resource or capability invalidates reuse.
12. Approval evidence has explicit issuance/expiry semantics and can be revoked. Expired or revoked evidence cannot satisfy a current review.
13. An agent/model cannot approve its own proposal, mint approval evidence, change its authenticated principal, or bypass required human/admin policy.
14. Review lookup/retry semantics are deterministic and bounded. Any v0.3 retention may remain process-local; it must not be described as durable authorization state.
15. Public reviewed-plan output remains explicitly non-executable. No executor handle, Polkit grant, prepare record or `apply` conversion is exposed.
16. D-Bus/CLI/SDK surfaces remain adapters to transport-neutral authority semantics rather than owning policy, risk-classification or approval rules.

## Risk-classification boundary

v0.2 can prove generically that a no-change plan is `ReadOnly` and a non-empty system-state diff is at least `SystemMutation`; it cannot safely infer all domain-specific security/destructive implications. v0.3 therefore treats planner risk as a lower bound and adds an authority-owned classifier in Control.

The initial classifier is intentionally narrow and conservative:

- canonical systemd `active_state` changes are classified `SecuritySensitive` because arbitrary service start/stop can affect exposure, privilege boundaries or availability;
- no other mutation route is guessed into a weaker class;
- a route/change shape without a trusted rule becomes `blocked`;
- a trusted rule result lower than the planner risk floor becomes `blocked`;
- overlapping rules select the highest risk deterministically;
- destructive classification is representable and routes to the destructive approval class when a trusted rule produces it, but v0.3 does not claim a production destructive mutation rule or supported destructive mutation.

These are review semantics only. Classification does not imply executor support or managed mutation support.

## Exact review binding

At minimum, every policy evaluation and approval check is correlated with:

```text
authenticated principal
request id
plan id
canonical planned changes/findings
observed evidence id
provider + resource + capability
semantic provenance
planner risk floor
classified prospective risk
risk-policy revision + matched rules
policy id + policy revision
approval requirement/evidence when applicable
```

`PlanId` alone is not sufficient authority evidence. In particular, v0.2 currently derives the plan ID from the request identity; therefore v0.3 must also bind current evidence, trusted risk classification and policy identity, and must reject any review whose material plan content no longer matches the reviewed subject.

## Determinism contract

For identical normalized review inputs — authenticated principal, canonical reconciliation plan, authoritative evidence identity, trusted risk-policy revision/rules and policy snapshot — risk classification, policy evaluation and the reviewed-plan result must be identical.

Wall-clock time may participate only through explicit bounded approval issuance/expiry checks. It must not alter the underlying deterministic risk classification or policy decision for the same snapshots and review subject.

## Trust-boundary invariants

- The authenticated principal is derived by the trusted local transport/control boundary; it is not supplied by the client payload.
- `Actor` remains provenance about who/what requested the work; it is not a substitute for authenticated principal identity.
- Models are untrusted proposers and never become policy, risk-classification or approval authorities.
- Policy/risk evaluation does not call providers, executors, Polkit, shells or model providers.
- Unknown/unclassified mutation risk and attempted risk downgrade fail closed.
- Approval does not create execution authority.
- `allow` does not create execution authority.
- A reviewed plan does not create a durable prepare record.
- Unknown policy, unknown approval class, missing evidence, stale review binding, expired approval and revoked approval fail closed.
- Review never upgrades cached/retrieved context into authoritative observation.
- The canonical eleven-stage lifecycle is unchanged; v0.3 proves only the `authorize`-side review boundary and deliberately stops before `prepare`.

## Core acceptance cases

The deterministic core must prove at minimum:

1. a valid low-risk subject can return `allow`;
2. policy can return explicit `deny`;
3. system mutation can require interactive approval;
4. a canonical security-sensitive plan can require administrator approval;
5. a canonical plan classified destructive by a trusted test rule can require the destructive approval class without claiming supported destructive mutation;
6. a blocked plan returns `blocked` and cannot be approved;
7. an unclassified canonical mutation becomes `blocked`;
8. a risk classification below the planner floor becomes `blocked`;
9. overlapping risk rules choose the highest risk deterministically;
10. an agent cannot self-authorize a mutation;
11. review binds the authenticated principal;
12. review binds the exact plan/request and authoritative evidence identity;
13. review retains risk-policy revision/rule identity as material provenance;
14. a different policy revision cannot reuse a prior evaluation/approval;
15. a different provider/resource/capability cannot reuse prior approval;
16. expired approval is rejected;
17. revoked approval is rejected;
18. an unauthorized approver is rejected;
19. deterministic retry returns the same review result for the same normalized input;
20. an approved reviewed plan still reports no execution authority and performs no native Linux mutation.

## Persistence and recovery boundary

v0.3 may use bounded process-local review/approval retention to prove semantics, but it does not claim durable authorization state across daemon restart. Durable request/plan/review binding, prepare state, replay prevention across restart, corruption handling and indeterminate recovery belong to v0.4.

If process-local review evidence is lost, the caller must obtain a fresh authoritative observation, plan and review rather than reconstruct authority from stale client state.

## Compatibility boundary

All v0.3 contracts remain Experimental. They may evolve coherently while Linura is pre-1.0, subject to `contracts/stability.toml` and the repository's contract-change procedure.

Removing the unused bootstrap `ActionPlan`/`ActionRequest`/`PlanResponse`/generic `Provider::plan`/`MutationRuntime` scaffolding is an intentional Experimental contract cleanup: those types are not preserved as a legacy compatibility path because they conflict with the canonical v0.2 plan lineage and have no supported user capability.

## Explicit non-goals

v0.3 does **not** claim:

- durable approval or transaction persistence;
- a prepared mutation or intent-to-execute record;
- executable effect generation as a public product contract;
- Polkit authorization or privileged credential acquisition;
- any public `apply`, execute or mutate method;
- privileged executor integration;
- post-mutation verification;
- commit/audit/reconciliation of a real effect;
- complete eleven-stage managed mutation;
- natural-language/model interpretation;
- fleet/remote authority;
- complete risk rules for every future provider/domain;
- a supported Linux distribution, desktop, machine class, hardware profile or virtualization profile;
- production readiness.
